# Warren Buffett's Investment Philosophy

## Origin
Buffett's framework descends directly from **Benjamin Graham** (his teacher
at Columbia and author of *The Intelligent Investor* and *Security Analysis*),
refined by **Charlie Munger's** insistence on quality businesses over merely
cheap ones. The synthesis: **"a wonderful company at a fair price is far
better than a fair company at a wonderful price."**

## The Four Foundations

### 1. Fundamental Value
A stock is not a ticker — it is fractional ownership of a real business with
real cash flows. Value is determined by the discounted cash the business will
generate over its remaining life. Price will eventually converge to value;
in the short run, the market is a voting machine, in the long run a weighing
machine (Graham).

### 2. Circle of Competence
You don't have to be right about everything — you have to be right about the
few things you choose. Buffett famously avoided tech for decades because he
didn't understand the economics. The size of the circle matters far less than
knowing where its edges are. **"Risk comes from not knowing what you're doing."**

### 3. Margin of Safety
Always pay materially less than your conservative estimate of intrinsic
value. The gap absorbs estimation errors, bad luck, and unknown unknowns.
Graham's analogy: if you're building a bridge to carry 10,000-pound trucks,
build it to hold 30,000 pounds. The same applies to investing.

### 4. Patient Compounding
Time is the friend of the wonderful business and the enemy of the mediocre
one. Holding a 15% compounder for 20 years turns $1 into $16. Trading in and
out — even successfully — bleeds returns through taxes, frictions, and
mistakes. **"The stock market is designed to transfer money from the active
to the patient."**

## Decision Filters

When evaluating any business, ask:
1. Do I understand it? (circle of competence)
2. Does it have a durable competitive advantage? (moat)
3. Is management honest and capable? (stewardship)
4. Can I buy it at a sensible price with margin of safety? (valuation)

If all four are "yes" — buy with conviction and concentrate.
If any is "no" — pass. There is **no obligation to swing**.

## Famous Buffett Quotes (Operating Principles)

- "Rule No. 1: Never lose money. Rule No. 2: Never forget Rule No. 1."
- "Be fearful when others are greedy, and greedy when others are fearful."
- "Our favorite holding period is forever."
- "Price is what you pay. Value is what you get."
- "It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair
  company at a wonderful price."
- "The stock market is a device for transferring money from the impatient
  to the patient."
- "Risk comes from not knowing what you're doing."
- "Diversification is protection against ignorance. It makes little sense
  if you know what you are doing."
- "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre."
- "You only have to do a very few things right in your life so long as you
  don't do too many things wrong."
- "The most important quality for an investor is temperament, not intellect."

## What Buffett Avoids
- Businesses he doesn't understand (long-time avoidance of unprofitable tech).
- High-leverage balance sheets.
- Serial acquirers and empire-builders.
- Businesses with poor unit economics dressed up as "growth stories."
- Frequent trading — turnover is the enemy.
- Forecasting macro variables (rates, GDP, FX) as a basis for investing.
- Following the crowd. Conviction comes from analysis, not consensus.
