# Trading Strategy 2 — Warren Buffett (Value Investing)

## Philosophy
Direct adoption of Warren Buffett's investment philosophy, heavily influenced by
Benjamin Graham. Focus: **value investing** — buy high-quality, durable
businesses at a reasonable price and hold them for the long term.

Four pillars:
1. **Fundamental value** — understand what the business is actually worth.
2. **Circle of competence** — only buy what you can understand.
3. **Margin of safety** — pay materially less than intrinsic value.
4. **Patient compounding** — hold for years, let compounding do the work.

> "Our favorite holding period is forever." — Warren Buffett
> "Price is what you pay. Value is what you get." — Warren Buffett
> "Be fearful when others are greedy, and greedy when others are fearful."

## Mission
Beat the S&P 500 (benchmark: SPY) on a risk-adjusted basis through long-term
ownership of wonderful businesses purchased at fair prices. Stocks only — no
options, ever.

## Capital & Constraints
- Starting capital: ~$100,000 (Alpaca paper)
- Platform: Alpaca (paper by default)
- Instruments: Common stocks ONLY (no options, no derivatives)
- Concentration > diversification — Buffett: "Diversification is protection
  against ignorance."

## Core Rules

### Selection (the "wonderful business" filter)
1. **Circle of competence** — if you can't explain the business in two
   sentences, skip it. No biotech moonshots, no opaque financial engineering,
   no businesses you wouldn't bet your house on understanding.
2. **Durable competitive moat** — brand, switching costs, network effects,
   cost advantages, regulatory capture. Must survive 10+ years of competition.
3. **Predictable earnings** — consistent ROE > 15% over the last 10 years,
   stable or growing free cash flow, low cyclicality.
4. **Honest, capable management** — rational capital allocators, candid in
   shareholder letters, owner-mindset, no empire-building.
5. **Strong balance sheet** — manageable debt (long-term debt < 3x annual
   earnings), no reliance on continuous capital raises.

### Valuation (the "fair price" filter)
6. **Margin of safety ≥ 25%** — only buy when market price is at least 25%
   below your conservative estimate of intrinsic value (DCF or owner-earnings
   yield vs. long bond).
7. **Reasonable multiples** — P/E generally < 20 for the business quality,
   P/FCF preferred over P/E, EV/EBIT as cross-check.
8. **Owner earnings yield > 10-yr Treasury + risk premium** — if a business
   doesn't beat the risk-free rate by a comfortable margin, pass.

### Position Management
9. **Concentration**: 5-8 positions max. High conviction, large weights.
10. **Position size**: up to 25% per position for highest-conviction ideas;
    typical 10-15%.
11. **Capital deployed**: 60-95%. Cash is a position — hold it when nothing
    is cheap. Never force a trade.
12. **Holding period**: minimum 3 years intended; default forever.
13. **No trailing stops on quality** — Buffett doesn't sell wonderful
    businesses on a 10% pullback. Volatility is opportunity, not risk.
    *(Deviation from Strategy 1 — intentional.)*
14. **Sell only when**: (a) thesis broken (moat eroded, management
    deteriorated, fundamentals decay), (b) price wildly above intrinsic
    value (>50% premium), or (c) better opportunity demands the capital.

### Discipline
15. **Patience > activity** — "The stock market is a device for transferring
    money from the impatient to the patient."
16. **Ignore Mr. Market** — daily price quotes are noise. Re-underwrite the
    thesis quarterly on earnings, not on price moves.
17. **No leverage, no shorts, no options.** Ever.
18. **Inactivity bordering on sloth** is the correct default. If no
    qualifying opportunity exists, do nothing.

## Entry Checklist (every prospective buy)
- [ ] Inside circle of competence — can you explain it in 2 sentences?
- [ ] Durable moat identified and named (which type?)
- [ ] 10-yr ROE > 15%, FCF positive every year?
- [ ] Long-term debt < 3x earnings?
- [ ] Management quality: read last 3 annual letters — rational? candid?
- [ ] Intrinsic value calculated (DCF / owner earnings)
- [ ] Margin of safety ≥ 25%?
- [ ] Would you be happy if the market closed for 5 years after you buy?

If any answer is "no" or "unsure" — **pass**. There is no penalty for
inaction. The penalty is for permanent loss of capital.

## Exit Checklist (every prospective sell)
- [ ] Has the moat been breached or eroded?
- [ ] Has management materially changed for the worse?
- [ ] Have the fundamentals (ROE, FCF, margins) durably deteriorated?
- [ ] Is the price >50% above your updated intrinsic value estimate?
- [ ] Is there a clearly superior opportunity needing this capital?

If all answers are "no" — **hold**. Volatility alone is never a reason to sell.

## What This Strategy Will NOT Do
- Will not chase momentum or sector rotation.
- Will not trade earnings, news, or technical signals.
- Will not use trailing stops as automated risk management on quality names.
- Will not hold cyclicals at peak earnings.
- Will not buy what it does not understand, regardless of how much it's up.
